# DBIA – Dairy Business Builder Grant (2026) — SOP

## 1. Grant Purpose

The Dairy Business Builder Grant promotes and strengthens the dairy industry within an
11-state region (Illinois, Iowa, Indiana, Kansas, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Nebraska,
Ohio, South Dakota, Wisconsin) by supporting dairy farm diversification, value-added dairy
product development, and market expansion. It's designed to improve profitability,
productivity, innovation, and job creation within dairy businesses.

## 2. Grant Overview

- **Funding type:** Competitive, reimbursement-based grant.
- **Award size:** Up to $150,000 per project. Large equipment expenditures may also be
  awarded up to $150,000 due to rising equipment costs.
- **Match requirement:** None — no matching funds required.
- **Cap per company:** One active project per applicant per cycle. Previous awardees may
  apply again only for a different project or project phase.
- **Project period:** Tied to the award cycle; costs must be incurred and completed within
  the funded project period.

## 3. Reimbursement Rules

This is a reimbursement-type grant — applicants must pay project costs upfront and are
reimbursed upon submission of valid documentation (canceled checks, paid invoices).
Reimbursement for costs incurred *before* the award is not allowed under any
circumstances. Applicants must have the cash flow to front 100% of project costs before
DBIA funds arrive.

## 4. Eligible Projects

Projects must align with at least one DBIA goal:

- Dairy farm diversification (product development, specialization, packaging, marketing).
- Creation of value-added dairy products (e.g., cheese, yogurt, ice cream, beverages).
- Enhancement of a dairy commodity or by-product through new uses or product development
  (e.g., converting whey permeate into a higher-value product).
- Creation or expansion of dairy export programs.

Strong projects also demonstrate measurable business impact: increased profitability or
productivity, new products or improved processes, market expansion or new sales outlets,
job creation or retention, and increased transportation/logistical efficiency.

## 5. Eligible Costs

- Equipment purchases directly tied to an eligible project category above.
- Rental of land or building space, provided the lease ends within the grant period.

## 6. Ineligible Costs

- Purchasing land, buildings, or construction/renovation of any kind.
- Lease-to-own arrangements (not treated as a rental).
- Any cost incurred or purchase made before the award is granted.

## 7. Most Important Grant Rules

- **Do not purchase anything before the award is announced** — this is the single most
  common way an otherwise-eligible applicant loses reimbursement eligibility.
- Milk used in beverage fluid milk products must meet Grade A requirements.
- No immediate family members of the applicant may work for the Center for Dairy Research
  (CDR), Wisconsin Cheese Makers Association (WCMA), or serve on the review committee.
- Applicants with an active DBIA grant that is less than 50% reimbursed are **not**
  eligible this cycle.
- Previous DBIA awardees must apply for a different project or project phase and
  demonstrate prior success — reapplying for the same project is disqualifying.
- Applicants must be in good standing with the IRS, with an EIN, at time of award.

## 8. Applicant Eligibility Questions

**Business**
- Are you a dairy farm or dairy manufacturing/processing business?
- Is your business address and manufacturing location in an eligible state?
- Are you a for-profit entity?
- Do you have an active EIN and are you in good standing with the IRS?

**Previous Funding**
- Have you ever applied for a DBIA grant before?
- Have you ever won a DBIA grant before? If so, is this a genuinely new project or phase?
- Do you have any active DBIA grants? If so, are they at least 50% reimbursed?

**Compliance**
- Does your milk meet Grade A requirements (if applicable to your product)?
- Do any immediate family members work for CDR, WCMA, or sit on the review committee?
- Do you meet all applicable state and federal regulations for your operation?

## 9. Project Eligibility Questions

- What would you use the grant funds for, specifically?
- Does the project align with at least one DBIA goal (diversification, value-added,
  commodity enhancement, or export expansion)?
- Do you have a confirmed project location?
- Do you hold the licenses required for this specific project?
- Is the project feasible to complete within the grant's project period?

## 10. Evaluation Criteria

DBIA does not publish a fixed public rubric, so Lasso scores applications internally
against the factors reviewers consistently reward. Score each 1–10 and weigh the
overall competitiveness holistically rather than as a strict sum:

- **Alignment with DBIA Goals** — how directly the project maps to diversification,
  value-added production, commodity enhancement, or export expansion.
- **Financial Transparency & Readiness** — clear budget, quotes in hand, demonstrated
  ability to pay upfront and wait for reimbursement.
- **Measurable Impact** — quantified outcomes (jobs created/retained, revenue growth,
  units/volume produced) rather than vague claims of benefit.
- **Execution Speed** — ability to source equipment/supplies and complete the project
  quickly once awarded.
- **Track Record** — prior DBIA awards reimbursed successfully, or a clean first-time
  application with no red flags.

## 11. Questions That Strengthen the Application

- Do you have signed vendor quotes rather than rough estimates?
- Can you quantify job creation/retention or revenue impact with real numbers, not
  generalities?
- Do you have a finalized (not draft) business plan?
- Can you demonstrate community or market demand for the resulting product?
- Is there a compelling differentiation angle (e.g., clean-label, organic, regional
  sourcing) that a reviewer would find memorable?

## 12. Project Readiness Checklist

- [ ] Business plan finalized (not just drafted)
- [ ] Vendor quotes or cost estimates obtained for all major equipment/purchases
- [ ] Bank letter or other proof of ability to pay costs upfront
- [ ] Letters of support secured from community/industry partners
- [ ] All required licenses and permits confirmed in place
- [ ] Confirmed the project has not started and no purchases have been made yet

## 13. Documents to Request

**Business**
- EIN verification / W-9
- Business license or registration
- Organizational structure (LLC, cooperative, etc.)

**Financial**
- Bank letter confirming ability to pay costs upfront
- Most recent tax return or financial statement

**Project**
- Vendor quotes or written cost estimates
- Project timeline/workplan
- Site control documentation (lease or deed), if applicable

**Supporting**
- Letters of support from community, industry, or financial partners
- Business plan (final version)

## 14. Financing Questions

- Do you have sufficient cash on hand to cover 100% of project costs before
  reimbursement arrives?
- If not, do you have a line of credit or other financing lined up to bridge the gap?
- Have you budgeted for the time lag between paying invoices and receiving
  reimbursement?

## 15. Client Commitment Questions

- Are you prepared to hold off on all purchases until the award is officially announced?
- Can you commit to gathering and submitting reimbursement documentation (receipts,
  canceled checks, invoices) promptly after each purchase?
- Are you available for a follow-up call if DBIA or Lasso needs clarification during
  review?

## 16. Red Flags

- Applicant has already made purchases related to the project before applying.
- Applicant has an active DBIA grant less than 50% reimbursed.
- Applicant cannot demonstrate the cash flow to pay upfront.
- Project has no quantifiable outcomes and reads as vague or generic.
- Applicant is reapplying for the same project/phase they were previously awarded for.
